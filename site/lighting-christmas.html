The arrangement

You are not buying lights.
You are buying the season.

The lighting stays ours. Everything that usually makes holiday lighting a chore stays ours too.

What you never deal with again

  • Buying a setNo upfront purchase, and no replacing a display that has faded to three different shades of white.
  • Getting on a ladderIn November, on a roofline, in Northern Ontario. We would rather it was us.
  • Storing itNothing takes up a shelf in your garage for ten months of the year.
  • A bulb failingIf something in your stored set stops working, that is ours to fix. It gets repaired or replaced before it goes back up.

What you get each year

  • The same display, fitted to your houseCut and laid out for your roofline the first year, then reused. It fits because it was made to.
  • A cheaper second seasonThe first year covers the design and the set. After that you are paying for installation and removal, not for building it again.
  • Modular, so it is repairableWe use commercial-grade modular lighting specifically because a failed section can be replaced instead of the whole run.
  • Two visits and nothing elseOne to install, one to remove. You do not need to be home for either.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

So I do not own the lights?

Correct — they stay ours, and that is deliberate. It is why you are not buying a set, not storing it, and not paying for it when a section fails. Your display is kept under your name and goes back on your house next season.

What if I stop after one year?

Then we collect the set at removal and that is the end of it. You are never left with equipment you did not want or a contract for seasons you have not asked for.

Can I have permanent lighting instead?

Plenty of people move to it after a season or two, because it covers the holidays and every other night of the year. Have a look at permanent lighting.
